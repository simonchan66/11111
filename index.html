THE PASSING RULE · PP. 12–13, 18

All seven must pass.

7 / 7

Every clinical requirement must reach at least the minimally competent category.

0 major errors

Any listed Fail-category error fails the Clinical Skills component. Strength elsewhere does not offset it.

≤ 2 safety errors

Three or more infection-control/safety errors, or one critical safety error, fails the component.

A passing result need not be ideal. Minor defects may be accepted only under the conditions stated below. These are tolerance limits, not preparation targets; correct avoidable defects before submission.

01

TASK 1 · PROTOCOL P. 20

Class II amalgam preparation

Preserve tooth strength while creating adequate resistance and retention.

Minimum passing outcome

Pulpal depth
1.0 mm ≤ depth < 3.0 mm. Ideal: 1.5 mm ≤ depth < 2.0 mm.
Gingival floor width (mesiodistal)
Premolar: 1.0 mm ≤ width < 2.0 mm. Molar: 1.0 mm ≤ width < 3.0 mm.
Adjacent-tooth clearance
Both proximal and gingival margins must clear the adjacent tooth. Proximal clearance must be > 0 and < 1.5 mm. Gingival clearance: ideal 0.5 mm; listed minor overclearance > 0.5 and < 2.0 mm. The gingival margin must be apical to the contact area and not subgingival. Ideal: > 0.5 mm supragingival; ≤ 0.5 mm supragingival is minimally competent.
Form and finish
Smallest supplied condenser must fit. Excess buccolingual width may pass only if convenience and resistance remain adequate. Preserve an axial wall separating pulpal and gingival floors. A sharp internal line angle, minor roughness or unsupported enamel may pass only if structural/marginal integrity remains sound.

Major errors · any one fails

  • Proximal clearance ≥ 1.5 mm; gingival clearance ≥ 2.0 mm; either margin fails to clear the adjacent tooth; subgingival gingival margin.
  • Depth < 1.0 mm or ≥ 3.0 mm; gingival floor width below 1.0 mm or at/above the upper limits.
  • Divergent proximal-box walls, flared buccal/lingual box walls, or occlusal divergence compromising retention.
  • Absent axial wall (no distinction between pulpal and gingival floors); excessive cutting; occlusal unsupported enamel from overpreparation; roughness/unsupported enamel compromising integrity.
  • Tooth damage requiring restoration, excessive soft-tissue injury, or repairing examination-tooth or adjacent-tooth structure with restorative material.
Critical errors

Wrong tooth; no preparation on assigned tooth; incorrect preparation design; evaluation prevented.

02

TASK 2 · PROTOCOL P. 21

Class II amalgam restoration

Restore contact, margins and function without a replacement-level defect.

Minimum passing outcome

Margins
A small deficiency manageable by enameloplasty may pass. Excess amalgam may pass only if gingival health is unaffected.
Proximal contour
Axial overcontour/undercontour may pass if gingival health is not negatively affected. The 2026 rubric gives no numerical axial-contour tolerance.
Contact
Contact must prevent food impaction and permit a patient to pass unwaxed floss. A contact located too occlusally or too gingivally is listed as minimally competent; a light contact that could cause food impaction fails.
Occlusion and finish
Minor occlusal contour errors may pass if contacts/function remain acceptable. Mild supraocclusion may pass with light contacts on other teeth in the same quadrant. Roughness must be polishable without changing contours.

Major errors · any one fails

  • Marginal deficiency/void requiring replacement; excess amalgam harming gingiva.
  • Absent or light contact that could cause food impaction; contact preventing a patient from passing unwaxed floss; contour harming gingival health.
  • Traumatic or fracture-risk supraocclusion; restoration too thin for function.
  • Roughness whose correction would harm contours; nonmarginal surface voids/porosity compromising durability or preventing proper oral hygiene.
  • Tooth damage requiring restoration, excessive tissue injury, or repairing examination-tooth or adjacent-tooth damage with restorative material.
Critical errors

Wrong tooth; no restoration; incorrect/inappropriate material; fractured or loose restoration; evaluation prevented.

03

TASK 3 · PROTOCOL P. 22

Class II composite resin restoration

Deliver a fully polymerized, durable posterior restoration with usable contact.

Minimum passing outcome

Margins
Deficiency or excess resin may pass when correctable without replacing the restoration.
Proximal contour and contact
Axial contour variation may pass if gingival health is unaffected; no numerical contour tolerance is given. Contact located too occlusally or too gingivally may pass. Absent or light contact that could cause food impaction fails; a patient must be able to pass unwaxed floss.
Occlusion
Contour variation must not impair contacts in maximum intercuspation or function. Mild supraocclusion may pass with light contacts on other teeth in the same quadrant.
Finish and resin quality
Roughness must be polishable without changing contours. Correctable contamination is listed as minimally competent; full polymerization and structural durability are essential.

Major errors · any one fails

  • Marginal deficiency/void or excess resin requiring replacement.
  • Absent or light proximal contact that could cause food impaction; contact a patient cannot floss; proximal contour harming gingiva.
  • Traumatic/fracture-risk occlusion or an occlusal surface too thin for function.
  • Roughness whose correction would harm contours; nonmarginal internal/surface voids or porosity compromising esthetics/durability or preventing proper oral hygiene; incomplete polymerization.
  • Contamination requiring complete replacement; tooth damage needing restoration; excessive tissue injury; repairing examination-tooth or adjacent-tooth structure with restorative material.
Critical errors

Wrong tooth; no restoration; incorrect/inappropriate material; fractured or loose restoration; evaluation prevented.

04

TASK 4 · PROTOCOL P. 23

Class IV composite resin restoration

Restore anterior form, contact and function with acceptable esthetics.

Minimum passing outcome

Margins
Small deficiencies or excess resin may pass if correctable without replacement.
Proximal contour and contact
Minor proximal, facial or lingual contour imperfections with only minimal gingival/esthetic impact may pass. Contact located too incisally or too gingivally may pass. Absent or light contact that could cause food impaction fails; a patient must be able to pass unwaxed floss. No numerical contour tolerance is given.
Incisal, labial and lingual form
Maintain acceptable incisal-edge harmony and natural form. Significant esthetic harm fails. Facial and lingual contour variations are assessed under the contour criterion.
Occlusion and finish
Mild supraocclusion may pass if other teeth retain light contacts, but traumatic or fracture-risk contact fails. Polishable roughness that preserves contours and correctable contamination are listed as minimally competent.

Major errors · any one fails

  • Marginal deficiency/void or excess resin requiring replacement.
  • Absent or light contact that could cause food impaction; contact a patient cannot floss; contour negatively affecting gingiva or esthetics.
  • Traumatic/fracture-risk occlusion; incisal-edge disharmony harming esthetics.
  • Contour-altering roughness; porosity/voids harming esthetics or durability; incomplete polymerization; contamination requiring entire replacement.
  • Tooth damage requiring restoration, excessive tissue injury, or repairing examination-tooth or adjacent-tooth damage with restorative material.
Critical errors

Wrong tooth; no restoration; incorrect/inappropriate material; fractured or loose restoration; evaluation prevented.

05

TASK 5 · PROTOCOL P. 24

Endodontic access cavity preparation

Expose the chamber and every canal while preserving the tooth.

Minimum passing outcome

Access
Open the pulp chamber. The access outline must permit access to every canal; do not restrict straight-line access to any canal.
Chamber roof and pulp horns
Remove the chamber roof and pulp horns without perforation. Minimal internal-wall gouging may pass only if structural integrity is preserved.
Canal access depth
Ideal: 2.0 mm. Less than 2.0 mm is explicitly listed as minimally competent when access/instrumentation is adequate. Overinstrumentation may pass only if prognosis is unaffected.
Cleanliness and finish
Minimal pulp remnants or debris may pass only if canals remain accessible. Rough margins must be correctable without weakening the tooth or its later restoration.

Major errors · any one fails

  • Chamber not accessed; access position/shape restricting straight-line access to any canal.
  • Outline extending beyond a cusp ridge, marginal ridge, incisal edge or cingulum; major canal obstruction; perforation.
  • Unnecessary internal cutting or examination-tooth damage that changes the restorative treatment plan.
  • Excess pulp remnants; debris obstructing chamber or any canal; roughness whose correction weakens tooth/restoration.
  • Rebuilding/repairing tooth structure with restorative material; adjacent damage requiring restoration; excessive tissue injury.
Critical errors

Access started on wrong tooth; no preparation on assigned tooth; evaluation prevented.

06

TASK 6 · PROTOCOL P. 25

Crown preparation

Allow fabrication and insertion of the specified crown with adequate retention.

Minimum passing outcome

Draw and retention
Crown must insert and withdraw. Extra convergence or parallel walls may pass if retention/draw remains acceptable. An undercut may pass only if spacer alone blocks it, without further preparation or composite buildup.
Reduction
Provide clearance for the specified restoration; preserve vitality, tooth strength and crown retention. Clearance is assessed in maximum intercuspation. The protocol does not give one universal occlusal-reduction or taper target.
Margin position
Ideal: 0.5 mm supragingival. Listed passing positions extend from the gingival level to 1.0 mm supragingival, and up to 1.0 mm subgingival. A correct, continuous, discernible margin must follow gingival contour.
Axial reduction at margin
Facial reduction must be within 0.5 mm of the requirement for the specified restoration. Explicit fail cutoffs: facial < 0.5 mm; lingual < 0.3 mm; excessive axial reduction > 1.5 mm. These cutoffs alone do not guarantee a pass.
Finish
Minor roughness, sharp angles or unsupported enamel may pass only if fabrication, retention and structural/marginal integrity remain acceptable.

Major errors · any one fails

  • Blocked insertion/removal; retention-compromising convergence; undercut needing preparation modification.
  • Insufficient reduction preventing fabrication; reduction compromising vitality, strength or retention; incisal/occlusal reduction harming the occlusal plane or ignoring existing clearance.
  • Margin > 1.0 mm above or below gingiva; wrong margin design; margin not following gingival contour; discontinuous/indiscernible margin preventing acceptable fabrication.
  • Axial margin reduction outside explicit fail cutoffs, or an otherwise inadequate material-specific preparation.
  • Sharp angles/unsupported enamel compromising integrity; tooth damage requiring restoration; excessive tissue injury; rebuilding/repairing tooth structure with restorative material.
Critical errors

Wrong tooth; no preparation; evaluation prevented.

07

TASK 7 · PROTOCOL P. 26

Provisional crown restoration

Seat a stable, retentive and removable provisional on your own crown preparation.

Minimum passing outcome

Seat and adaptation
Fully seated, stable and retentive. The crown must also be removable.
Margins
Minor underextension may pass only if the marginal seal is unaffected; minor undercontour if durability is unaffected; minor overextension/overcontour if gingival health is unaffected.
Contour, contact and occlusion
Maintain acceptable anatomy and strength. Contact located too occlusally or too gingivally may pass; absent or light contact risking food impaction fails. A patient must be able to pass unwaxed floss. Mild supraocclusion may pass with light contacts on other teeth in the same quadrant.
Surface and integrity
Floss must not break. Minor roughness/porosity may pass if discomfort and food retention are unlikely. Cracked or locally thin areas may pass only if structural durability is unaffected; a submitted fracture is critical.

Major errors · any one fails

  • Underextension compromising the marginal seal; overcontour harming gingiva; overextension/undercontour weakening the crown.
  • Instability unrelated to preparation design; crown cannot be removed or seated.
  • Poor anatomy harming gingiva/esthetics or durability; absent or light contact risking food impaction; contact a patient cannot floss; traumatic/fracture-risk occlusion.
  • Fracture/crack likely to fail under normal function; roughness breaking floss; roughness/porosity causing discomfort or food retention.
  • Excess restorative material on/in hard or soft tissue.
Critical errors

No provisional; submitted provisional fractured; evaluation of provisional prevented.

Damage: the shared limit

Minor examination-tooth or adjacent-tooth damage is acceptable only when enameloplasty is sufficient; minor soft-tissue injury is listed as minimally competent. Major damage fails. This shared rule applies to Tasks 1–6, with stricter restorative-treatment-plan consequences for endodontic access.

“Correctable” does not authorize repairing tooth damage with restorative material. Several tasks explicitly list that repair as a failing error.

THROUGHOUT THE DAY · PP. 13–15

Infection control & safety

Maximum two noncritical errors across the component. Morning and afternoon are assessed separately; repeating the same error in the afternoon counts again.

Prevent ordinary errors

  • Perform hand hygiene; wear mask and eye protection correctly; control hair; replace torn gloves.
  • Do not touch face, hair, mask, clothing, battery or loupes adjustment cord with treatment gloves.
  • Prevent contaminated surfaces, supplies, instruments and equipment; protect supplies from aerosols; keep bulk containers off working surfaces.
  • Use designated forceps or proper infection-control measures for cabinet supplies.
  • Handle instruments, sharps and amalgam safely; dispose of amalgam correctly; use sufficient high-speed cooling water.
  • Treat the manikin as a patient: no leaning/contact on the torso or head; do not damage the oral-cavity cover.
  • Remove foreign objects, restorative devices and materials related to the examination tooth.

Critical safety errors

  • No mask, no gloves, or no eye protection.
  • Loose supplies left in the oral cavity at the end that clearly threaten patient safety, such as burs, files or matrix bands.
  • Clear failure to understand infection-control and safety principles.

Barrier rule

Only the supplied stool/light barrier-covered areas may be touched with treatment gloves. Other stool/light areas require proper hand hygiene. Do not add barriers.

Exam-specific glove exceptions: manikin head lever/head, torso, typodont excursion hooks, amalgamator, cabinet top opening/closing, provisional box placement/closure, loupes/headlight (excluding battery/cord), water/air knobs, saliva-ejector switch and cabinet waste bag.

EXECUTION NOTES · PP. 6, 8–10

Before you submit

  • Verify assigned tooth using FDI numbering, task design and specified material.
  • Inspect margins, anatomy, contact strength, patient-flossable contacts and occlusion; ensure all seven requirements are evaluable.
  • Fabricate the provisional on your own crown preparation. Tasks may be performed in any order.
  • A dental dam is not required for these examination tasks in this edition; accepted clinical-care and safety standards still apply.
  • Complete any Communication Form during examination time; report supplied-equipment problems immediately to an invigilator.
  • Attend orientation on time; do not set up during orientation. Take the mandatory 30-minute lounge break. Unscheduled breaks receive no extra time, and leaving the Test Centre ends your access.
  • Bring only permitted eye protection, magnification, headlight/batteries or charger, approved personal items and lunch (kept in the lounge). No watches, rings or transmitting devices.
  • Bring current original government-issued photo ID in English or French, with your exact profile name. Photocopies and digital images are not accepted.
